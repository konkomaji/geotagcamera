Every photo, exactly where and when it happened.

GeoTag Camera is an Android camera that stamps the GPS location, address and time onto each photo, and signs the photo on the phone so anyone can later check it hasn't been edited. I built it for surveyors, inspectors, students and NGO teams who need proof, not clutter.

Why this exists

Most geotagging apps get the basics wrong

Every app I tried wanted something before it let me take a photo. A video ad before the camera opened. A subscription to remove a stamp field I didn't want. Permissions that have nothing to do with a camera. And the moment there was no signal, half of it stopped working, which is exactly where field work happens.

So I wrote my own and published the code. It does one job: a photo that shows where and when it was taken, that you can check later.

1. Frame it

The stamp is drawn live on the viewfinder by the same code that burns it into the photo, so what you frame is what you get.

2. Shoot it

Location, address and time go onto the image and into its EXIF data. The photo is hashed and signed on the phone and saved to your normal gallery.

3. Prove it

Anyone with the app can verify the photo later, offline. Export the locations or build a PDF site report when the job is done.

What's inside

Everything a proof-of-location photo needs

A compact stamp, signed at capture

Place, address with Plus Code, coordinates, date and time with GMT offset, plus accuracy, altitude and bearing if you want them. Every line stays on one line, so the Bar stamp covers about a sixth of a portrait photo. Or pick the small Minimal label.

A map drawn on the phone

The stamp map comes from OpenStreetMap, rendered on the device with MapLibre and OpenFreeMap tiles. No API key, no account. Places you've shot in stay available offline, and you can save a 2 to 30 km area before a trip.

Works without signal

Capturing, stamping, signing and verifying need no connection. With no signal the stamp uses the nearest address already cached on the phone, and the Plus Code is always worked out on the device. No GPS fix yet? The photo waits for one instead of being lost.

Projects for each site or job

Pick a project under the viewfinder and photos go into it. Filter the gallery by project, move photos between projects, rename or archive them. Deleting a project never deletes photos.

A note and your own text on the stamp

A sticky note such as "Pole 12, north wall" is burned into every photo until you change it. A fixed custom line holds a contract number or an inspector's name.

Export to CSV, KML or GeoJSON

Export a project, a selection or everything, with time, place, note and each photo's SHA-256. CSV opens in Excel and Sheets, KML in Google Earth, GeoJSON in QGIS and ArcGIS.

A PDF site report

A cover with an overview map of numbered photo locations and an index, then two photos per page with time, place, coordinates, note and a fresh verification result for each file.

Verify any photo

The proof travels inside the file, so the app can check a photo someone else sent you, on any phone, offline. The result shows the signing key's fingerprint and whether it is your own phone's key.

Import your photos back

Reinstalled the app or moved to a new phone? Gallery, Import photos re-adds GeoTag Camera photos already in your gallery to the in-app list. The proofs are inside the files, so they still verify.

A camera that fits your phone

Zoom presets from the real lenses, a flash button only when there is a flash, and the phone maker's Night, HDR and Portrait modes when it offers them. 4:3, 16:9, 1:1 or Full, and the saved photo matches the frame on screen.

Your organisation on the stamp

Add your college, company or project name and logo, and a hand-drawn signature inside the stamp. Professional mode can make the signature required. None of it is behind a paywall.

No ads, no trackers, no Google

No ad SDKs, no crash reporters, no account, and no analytics in the GitHub and F-Droid versions (the Google Play version asks first, and it's off unless you agree). Location comes from Android's own location services, so the app runs on phones without Google Play services.

How verification works

What the signature proves, and what it doesn't

  1. The stamp is burned in and the photo is saved as a JPEG.
  2. The image data is hashed with SHA-256. Metadata segments are left out, so the proof can be written into the file without changing the hash.
  3. The hash, latitude, longitude and time are signed with an ECDSA P-256 key created on your phone in the Android Keystore. The private key can't be exported.
  4. The hash, signature and public key are embedded in the photo's EXIF and XMP, so the proof goes wherever the file goes.

Verifying recomputes the hash, checks the signature against the embedded key, and checks the photo's EXIF location and time against what was signed. No network, no account.

> sha256(image data)

9f2c41d0b7e3...a81f5c

> sign(hash, lat, lon, time, keystoreKey)

MEUCIQD4x7...signature

> verify(photo.jpg)

Verified: image, location and time unchanged since signing

key fingerprint 4F1A 9C02 77DE B3E5

A "verified" result tells you

  • The pixels, including the burned-in stamp, haven't changed since signing.
  • The EXIF latitude, longitude and capture time still match what was signed.
  • Which key signed it, as a short fingerprint you can compare with one the photographer gives you.

It does not tell you

  • Who took the photo. The key isn't tied to a person; someone can strip the proof and re-sign an edit with their own key. That's what the fingerprint is for.
  • That the GPS or the clock were right. A spoofed fix is stamped like any other.
  • Anything about other metadata such as altitude or camera details, which isn't signed.

Messaging apps that recompress photos change the pixels, so the copy no longer verifies even though nobody tampered with it. Send the original file when it matters. The full details are in the README.

Who it's for

Two setups, picked on first launch

The app asks once which one fits you. You can switch in Settings any time, and nothing is locked in either mode.

Field

Students, NGO and field staff

You're recording a well, a school or a road in a village with no signal, or submitting fieldwork for a report. You want it quick and you can't pay for basics.

  • Simple stamp with place, address, coordinates and time
  • Fewer settings on screen
  • Optional signature, notes and projects when you need them

Professional

Surveyors and inspectors

You need a photo that shows it was taken on site, at that time, and a report the client accepts at the end of the job.

  • Altitude, accuracy and bearing on the stamp
  • Required signature before a photo is saved
  • Every setting visible, plus exports and PDF reports

Privacy

Your photos stay on your phone

I didn't build a server, so there's nothing for me to collect. These are the only times the app touches the network, and the privacy policy has the details.

  • No ads, analytics or trackers. No account, no cloud sync, no automatic crash reports.
  • Location only while the camera is open. Never in the background.
  • Map tiles from OpenFreeMap, for the area around you, cached on the phone. You can turn the map off.
  • Weather is off by default. If you turn it on, your position is rounded to about 1 km before it goes to Open-Meteo.
  • Addresses come from your phone's own geocoder, which on phones with Google services is usually Google's.
  • Kept out of cloud backups. Delete all data wipes the app's records; your gallery photos stay yours.
